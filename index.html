Who Am I?

I am a computer engineer and researcher who has spent my career looking for better ways to design and organize complex systems.

My work spans hardware architecture, fault-tolerant systems, reconfigurable computing, embedded systems, intelligent monitoring, aquaculture simulation, and efficient AI.

At first glance, these may look like unrelated areas. For me, they are different places where I have applied the same way of thinking: understand the complete system, find what is limiting it, and look for a simple solution.

I spent more than a decade in academia, combining research, teaching, system design, and supervision. Later, through industrial R&D, I applied the same approach to practical systems and production problems.

Today, I am particularly interested in research and R&D problems where system-level understanding can reveal solutions that are simpler, more efficient, or more effective than the obvious ones.

Finding Simple Solutions for Unseen Bottlenecks in Complex Systems

This is the principle that connects my work.

I do not usually begin by asking which technology to use. I begin by trying to understand how the whole system works.

Understand the system ↓ Find the unseen bottleneck ↓ Develop a simple idea ↓ Redesign the system ↓ Evaluate and improve it

The application changes from one project to another, but this way of thinking has remained consistent throughout my research and engineering work.

Reconfigurable & High-Performance Computing

One of the important steps in my research journey was a 2D pipeline architecture that I developed as part of my Ph.D. research.

The idea grew from looking at how computation could be organized and parallelized more effectively. By studying the structure of the pipeline and the relationship between computation and data movement, I moved toward a more general reconfigurable architecture. During my literature review, I discovered that a processing architecture with characteristics similar to what I was designing had already been introduced: Coarse-Grain Reconfigurable Architecture (CGRA).

The resulting architecture was implemented at a large scale, containing approximately 1.8 million transistors.

How I Work

I usually start with the system rather than with a particular technology.

I try to understand how the complete system behaves, how its parts interact, and where the real limitation comes from.

The BTA production-line experience is a good example. The line was already functioning, but its efficiency could be improved. By examining the organization of the whole process rather than focusing only on individual components, I was able to identify weak points and redesign the organization of the system.

I enjoy this kind of problem:

a system that works,
a limitation that is not immediately obvious,
and an opportunity to make it significantly better through a simple idea.

I am comfortable working across research, system analysis, architecture, design, experimentation, and implementation. In many of my own projects I have written and developed the complete implementation myself.

However, my strongest contribution is usually in understanding the problem, developing the core idea, designing the system, and guiding its development.
