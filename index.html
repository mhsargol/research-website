Who Am I?

I am a researcher and system designer with a background in computer architecture and a career focused on understanding and improving complex systems.

My work spans hardware architecture, fault-tolerant systems, reconfigurable computing, embedded systems, intelligent monitoring, aquaculture simulation, and efficient AI.

At first glance, these may look like unrelated areas. For me, they are different places where I have applied the same way of thinking: understand the complete system, find what is limiting it, and look for a simple solution.

I spent more than a decade in academia, combining research, teaching, system design, and supervision. Later, through industrial R&D, I applied the same approach to practical systems and production problems.

Today, I am particularly interested in research and R&D problems where system-level understanding can reveal solutions that are simpler, more efficient, or more effective than the obvious ones.

Finding Simple Solutions for Unseen Bottlenecks in Complex Systems

This sentence summarizes the principle that connects much of my research and engineering work.

Complex systems do not always need more complicated solutions. Sometimes the most important improvement comes from discovering a bottleneck that is hidden by the way the system has been designed or organized.

I usually try to understand the system as a whole before focusing on individual components. This often reveals opportunities that are difficult to see when each part is considered separately.

I do not usually begin by asking which technology to use. I begin by trying to understand how the whole system works.

Understand the system
↓
Find the unseen bottleneck
↓
Develop a simple idea
↓
Redesign the system
↓
Evaluate and improve it

The application domains have changed throughout my career, but this way of thinking has remained consistent—from fault-tolerant digital systems and computer architecture to reconfigurable computing, intelligent systems, aquaculture, and industrial R&D.

My Work

Industrial R&D & System Optimization

After my academic career, my work with BTA developed around two major roles: system optimizer/designer and researcher.

One project involved an existing production line that was already working properly, but whose overall efficiency could be improved. Instead of optimizing individual components, I examined the organization of the complete system, identified its weak points, and designed a new organization for the production process. The system was not broken. The bottleneck was hidden in how it worked as a whole.

This experience is a good example of the principle that connects much of my work: understanding the whole system can reveal a simple way to achieve a significant improvement.

Fault-Tolerant Systems

My early research focused on designing reliable digital systems using low-cost architectural approaches.

I worked on concurrent error detection, modular partitioning, online and offline testing, and fault simulation, exploring how reliability could be improved without unnecessarily increasing system complexity or cost.

Reconfigurable & High-Performance Computing

During my Ph.D. research in Computer Architecture, I developed a two-dimensional pipeline architecture for high-performance computation.

While studying how to extend and generalize the underlying idea, I encountered Coarse-Grain Reconfigurable Architectures (CGRA), which provided a broader architectural framework closely related to the direction of my work.

This led to the design and implementation of my own CGRA architecture, which became the central part of my Ph.D. research. The architecture was implemented at a large scale, containing approximately 1.8 million transistors.

Efficient AI & Intelligent Computing

My current research focuses on making neural-network inference more efficient for resource-constrained computing systems.

Rather than focusing only on the neural-network model, I study the inference system as a whole, looking for unnecessary computation and memory activity that can be reduced through system-level design.

Initial experimental results have already been obtained, and further experiments are currently being gathered. These results are being prepared for the first paper from this research, which I expect to submit within the coming months.

How I Work

I usually start with the system rather than with a particular technology.

I enjoy problems where the system already works, but something is limiting its performance, efficiency, reliability, or scalability. I try to understand the interactions between its components, find the underlying bottleneck, and develop the simplest effective solution.

I am comfortable moving between research, system analysis, architecture, design, experimentation, and implementation. I have personally developed complete implementations in several projects, but my strongest contribution is usually in problem formulation, research, system design, and technical direction.
