Aquaculture & Intelligent Systems

Background

Through my work with BTA, I became involved in aquaculture-related research and engineering projects. BTA produced feed for aquaculture. One of its needs was to create a small pilot aquaculture environment where its products could be tested before being sold and to provide additional technological support to customers.

Working with an established industrial company with real products, industrial activities, and international customers allowed me to approach aquaculture not simply as an academic subject, but as a complex real-world system involving biological, physical, sensing, computational, and operational components.

Smart Aquaculture Monitoring and Control

I developed a monitoring and control system for shrimp ponds and rainbow-trout ponds. The system involved:

  • Environmental monitoring
  • Environmental control
  • Sensing
  • Embedded/system engineering
  • Aquaculture
  • Practical industrial R&D

This project revealed a strong connection between my existing engineering background and technology-oriented aquaculture R&D.

Rainbow-Trout Aquaculture Emulator

I independently developed a Python-based emulator for rainbow-trout fish ponds. This was not simply a small simulation script. I collected fish-growth equations, fish-digestion models, environmental models, and other relevant equations from reference books and other sources, then implemented them in an interactive simulation environment.

The pond was divided into 10 × 10 × 10 cm³ cells. Each cell maintained environmental information, while individual fish maintained their own state. The simulation considered factors including:

  • Fish density
  • Neighboring cells and their characteristics
  • Temperature
  • Dissolved oxygen
  • Water contamination
  • Water flow
  • Fish growth and survival
  • Environmental stress

The system could simulate approximately 180 days at hourly resolution. I also incorporated real temperature fluctuations obtained from a weather station in the target city.

The output included information such as surviving fish, individual fish characteristics, temperature, oxygen, water contamination, and environmental conditions within pond cells.

I considered the emulator conceptually similar to GEM5 in one respect: both represent a complex system through models of individual components/states and simulate their behavior over time.

Industrial Validation

The emulator was tested using information from several BTA customer farms. This should not be described as conventional academic validation. It was an industrial R&D project, and the available validation came from real information associated with BTA customer farms.

This makes the project particularly relevant to organizations working on aquaculture, water systems, environmental modelling, sensing, simulation, decision-support systems, and industrial R&D.
