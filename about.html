About Me

Education

BSc — Hardware Engineering, Computer Engineering

University of Shahid Bahonar Kerman, Iran — 2006

Final Project: Implementation of a remote-control hardware system through a wired telephone line. This was developed before SMS communication was available in Iran, using key-press control codes to remotely operate a digital system.

MSc — Computer Architecture, Computer Engineering

University of Tehran, Iran — 2009

Thesis: Fault-Tolerant System Design

Developed an RT-level Verilog fault-simulation toolchain and established the fundamental fault-tolerance idea that later appeared across multiple publications.

PhD — Computer Architecture, Computer Engineering

University of Tehran, Iran — 2018

Dissertation: Energy reduction in coarse-grained reconfigurable Processors by reducing context-switching overhead

The PhD involved a large-scale CGRA research project, including architecture implementation, custom generation tools, simulation/modeling infrastructure, and architecture optimization, resulting in a research platform of approximately 1.8 million transistors.

2019–2023 — Head of Computer Engineering Department

University of Sistan and Baluchestan

During part of my PhD period, I continued teaching because there were courses that nobody else available at the university could teach. I taught courses including:

  • BSc: Basic Programming, Discrete Mathematics, Digital Circuit Design, Computer Architecture, Data Transmission, VLSI System Design, Microprocessors
  • MSc: Advanced Computer Architecture, Dependable System Design, Advanced VLSI System Design

My teaching materials were generally prepared in English because I found that easier and more efficient.

Laboratory Development

I redesigned the syllabi and helped renew equipment for three laboratories:

  • Digital Circuit Design Laboratory
  • Computer Architecture Laboratory
  • Microprocessor Laboratory

Curriculum Development

As Head of the Computer Engineering Department, I worked on the complete undergraduate Computer Engineering curriculum. Using curricula from the top three Iranian universities as major references, I selected and reworked an updated curriculum better suited to preparing students for professional employment and further academic study.

Student Supervision

I supervised roughly a dozen BSc and MSc students. Students knew that working with me was not easy — I double-checked their work carefully, expected them to understand what they were doing, and did not simply accept superficial results. As a result, the students who strongly trusted themselves and wanted to do serious projects were more likely to come to me.

Their projects included hardware implementation, wearable healthcare, drone-based routing, and other engineering projects.

Department Leadership Impact

As Head of the Computer Engineering Department, I had a significant effect on both the department and student output. Before my period of leadership, approximately 1–3 students per year were accepted into MSc programs across all universities. After the changes, approximately 3–5 students per year were accepted into MSc programs at universities among the top five in Iran.
