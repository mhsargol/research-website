VR-Based Training and Research Platform

Around 2024, I became involved in a part-time industrial R&D project as System Designer and Senior Engineer. The project is a multidisciplinary VR-based training and research platform designed to support 12 simultaneous participants.

The platform combines 12 individual acoustic cabins—each equipped with a VR headset and a custom omnidirectional movement platform—with a shared virtual environment, system coordination, monitoring, data management, and AI-assisted assessment.

The platform includes several interacting subsystems:

  • User cabins: Twelve individual training stations designed for simultaneous participation.
  • Metaverse server: Provides the shared virtual environment and synchronization of participants.
  • Coordination server: Coordinates communication and operation across the overall system.
  • Monitoring system: Supports supervision of participants, training events, and system activity.
  • Physiological and neurological data: Supports the acquisition and analysis of relevant participant data.
  • Database: Manages participant records and training-related information.
  • AI subsystem: Supports game scoring and assessment-related functions.

The development work is organized across four multidisciplinary teams: VR and Graphics, Physiological and Neurological Data, Physical Equipment, and AI and Data Analysis. Interface development is handled across the relevant teams rather than by a separate permanent team.

My responsibilities include:

  • Designing the overall system architecture and defining the target functions and technical requirements of its subsystems.
  • Defining standardized communication and interfacing protocols between modules and the overall system.
  • Coordinating the four multidisciplinary development teams and guiding their technical work.
  • Reviewing team outputs and helping ensure that implementations conform to the architecture, requirements, and defined protocols.
  • Designing the AI subsystem and implementing a Python-based game-scoring component.
  • Leading the system-level integration and synchronization work.

The standardized interfaces are intended to make the architecture modular: a subsystem can be developed or replaced independently, provided that it conforms to the agreed interfaces and communication protocols.

Project status: Subsystem development has progressed, while final system-level integration, installation, commissioning, and end-to-end testing remain pending the readiness of the physical site.

Some technical details cannot be disclosed due to project privacy and confidentiality requirements.

Wearable Healthcare Device

Around 2019/2020, one BSc student wanted to do a real hardware implementation as his final project. I proposed the idea of a low-cost wearable healthcare device and supervised/coached him throughout the design and implementation process.

The intended device had three important characteristics:

  • Wearable and comfortable enough for normal daily activity
  • Inexpensive
  • Able to notify a family member when an unexpected incident/emergency occurred

The device was designed and implemented over approximately eight months. A patent application was initiated, but the process was later discontinued after the student moved to Canada for PhD study.

The important point is that this was not simply "I supervised a student." I provided the idea, technical direction, supervision, and coaching throughout the hardware design and implementation.
